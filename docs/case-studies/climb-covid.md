In response to the COVID-19 pandemic caused by SARS-CoV-2, CLIMB set
up the **CLIMB-COVID** environment to allow the COVID-19 Genomics UK
(COG-UK) Consortium to rapidly analyse SARS-CoV-2 genomes for genomic
surveillance.

The COG-UK consortium brought together NHS organisations, UK public health
agencies, over 20 academic partners across the UK, and dedicated regional
sequencing centres including the Quadram Institute Bioscience (QIB) in Norwich. Its
aim was to deliver large-scale sequencing combined with clinical
information, to inform interventions and policy decisions.

CLIMB-COVID was built within the existing CLIMB infrastructure to provide
reproducible data analysis pipelines, computing and storage capacity to
the consortium, facilitating data sharing and collaboration between
researchers. Once sequence data and associated metadata was uploaded by
regional sequencing centres, it was harmonised and passed through
continuous integration to public databases.

This let the consortium rapidly investigate cases in hospitals and the
community to understand how the virus was spreading and evolving,
enabling analysis of **over two millions SARS-CoV-2 genomes** since inception —
supporting outbreak management and vaccine research.

COG-UK closed on 31 March 2023, and CLIMB-COVID has wound down on 31 March 2026.
However, the CLIMB infrastructure remains ready for any public health
surveillance need — capacity can be scaled rapidly to support a
national capability.

For more detail, see the [CLIMB-COVID publication](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8247108/).
