Rosalind Elsie Franklin

X-ray Crystallographer | Physical Chemist | Molecular Biologist
London, England | 25 July 1920 – 16 April 1958

Professional Summary

Renowned British X-ray crystallographer and physical chemist whose pioneering work was instrumental in understanding the molecular structures of DNA, RNA, viruses, coal, and graphite. Best known for her X-ray diffraction images of DNA, particularly Photo 51, which provided critical evidence for the discovery of the DNA double helix structure. Her meticulous experimental approach and expertise in X-ray crystallography revolutionized structural biology. Despite her groundbreaking contributions, her work was largely unrecognized during her lifetime. Franklin was a meticulous scientist who believed in letting experimental data guide conclusions rather than premature model building.

Professional Experience

Birkbeck College, University of London

1953–1958
Senior Scientist / Research Associate in Biophysics
London, UK
  • Led pioneering research on molecular structures of viruses under crystallographer John Desmond Bernal
  • Conducted groundbreaking X-ray crystallography studies on Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV), discovering that all virus particles were of the same length
  • Published seminal works on the helical arrangement of protein subunits in TMV
  • Coordinated research on spherical viruses including polio virus
  • Supervised research team including PhD students John Finch and Kenneth Holmes
  • Investigated RNA structure and its location within virus particles
  • Worked on turnip yellow mosaic virus and cucumber virus 4

King's College London

1951–1953
Research Associate
Medical Research Council's Biophysics Unit, London, UK
  • Discovered key properties of DNA that facilitated the correct description of the double helix structure
  • Produced high-quality X-ray diffraction images of DNA, including the landmark Photo 51
  • Discovered that DNA exists in two forms (A and B) depending on humidity levels
  • Demonstrated that phosphate groups lie on the outside of the DNA molecule
  • Developed innovative humidity-controlled camera chamber for X-ray experiments
  • Applied Patterson function to analyze X-ray pictures of DNA
  • Presented data at seminars, establishing DNA's helical structure with 2, 3, or 4 co-axial chains

Key Scientific Contributions

  • DNA Structure: Produced Photo 51, the clearest X-ray diffraction image of DNA at the time, providing crucial evidence for the double helix structure
  • Virus Research: Pioneered X-ray crystallography studies on Tobacco Mosaic Virus, discovering uniform particle length and helical protein arrangement
  • Coal Science: Advanced understanding of coal porosity, graphitisation, and carbon structure
  • Methodological Innovation: Developed humidity-controlled camera chambers for precise X-ray experiments
  • RNA Research: Investigated ribonucleic acid structure in virus particles
